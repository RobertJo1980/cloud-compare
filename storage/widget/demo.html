Demo of the embeddable widget — data from pricing.json, logic in calc.js, UI in cloud-cost-compare.js. Embed with one script tag and one element.

The meters, in plain language

In a blog embed, the widget’s “More →” links point here via its guide-base attribute.

Data stored
Rent on your bytes: a price per GB per month, averaged over the billing period. The only meter every provider charges.
Downloads (egress)
Moving data out to the internet. AWS, Azure and GCS charge ~$0.09–0.12/GB after a free 100 GB (GCS: US buckets only); Cloudflare R2 is always free; Backblaze B2 is free up to 3× what you store; Wasabi is free provided monthly downloads stay under what you store.
API requests
Every upload, list or download is an API call, usually priced per thousand. Writes cost ~10× reads. B2 and Wasabi don’t charge for requests at all. The per-request charge is the same wherever the call comes from — what changes with location is the data transfer: traffic to a VM in the same region is free, while downloads to the internet pay the egress rates above (this widget models the internet case).
Storage classes
Standard for data in active use; Infrequent for data touched about monthly; Archive for data you may never read. Cheaper classes add retrieval fees, minimum stays, and sometimes hours-long restore waits — so the widget prices every class and shows each provider at its cheapest for your workload. Heavy downloads push the winner back toward Standard. B2 and Wasabi have a single class.
Retrieval fees
Cooler classes charge per GB just to read data back, on top of egress — $0.01–0.05/GB. Offline archive tiers (S3 Glacier, Azure Archive) also take hours to restore.
Minimum stays
Cool/archive classes bill 30–365 days per object even if you delete early. Wasabi applies 90 days to everything and bills at least 1 TB/month; B2 has no minimums.
Redundancy
How many copies your rent buys, and where. S3 and GCS replicate across three or more zones at list price; Azure’s headline prices are LRS (one datacentre) and zone-redundant ZRS costs ~10–30% more with no Archive tier; R2, B2 and Wasabi don’t publish a multi-zone model. The “Zone-redundant” toggle applies all of this.
Region
The toggle only shows providers with datacentres in that place — Backblaze B2 drops out of the UK view (its nearest region is Amsterdam) but returns in the EU view, and Cloudflare R2 carries a badge in the UK because it places data by “location hint” without a hard UK residency guarantee — its EU jurisdiction, by contrast, is a real guarantee. On price: AWS and GCS charge more in London than in US or EU regions (up to ~2× on archive tiers); Azure UK South is slightly cheaper than East US for hot data. R2, B2 and Wasabi use one global price list.
